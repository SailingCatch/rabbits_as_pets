A data story

Pet or pest? Keeping a rabbit in Australia

Australians love pets, yet few keep a rabbit. The reason: here, the rabbit is also the country's most costly pest.

  • 73% of Australian households have a pet
  • 1 in 65 households keeps a rabbit
  • 600 million wild rabbits at their peak, in 1950

Part one

The pet

01 · A nation of pet lovers

We love pets more than ever

Three in four households now have a pet, up from six in ten before COVID-19.

Dogs, cats, fish… and hardly any rabbits

Only 3% of households keep small mammals, and about half of those keep rabbits.

So why so few? In Australia, the rabbit was a pest long before it was a pet.

Part two

The pest

02 · A plague

From a few rabbits to 600 million

Released for hunting in 1859, rabbits covered 70% of the continent within about 70 years.

Fences didn't stop them

WA built three rabbit-proof fences in 1902–07. Wild rabbits are seen on both sides today.

03 · The virus war

Every new virus knocks them down. They come back.

Next came disease: myxomatosis in 1950, calicivirus in 1995, RHDV2 in 2015.

RHDV2, 2015

It crossed the continent in 18 months. At six monitoring sites, numbers fell 64% on average.

04 · Still everywhere

Wild rabbits live where people live

They're still common across the south, where most pet rabbits live too.

Part three

Back to the pet

05 · The rules

1 in 5 Australians can't legally keep a rabbit

Because rabbits are a pest, each state sets its own rules.

Banned in Queensland

SA requires an escape-proof cage; the ACT has a mandatory code.

06 · The risk at home

The viruses meant for wild rabbits reach pets too

Insects carry these viruses to backyard hutches. There's a calicivirus vaccine, but none for myxomatosis.

07 · When it goes wrong

When a small pet ends up in a shelter

The RSPCA counts rabbits with other small pets such as guinea pigs and ferrets.

More than 6,000 a year

Most arrive in NSW, Victoria and Queensland.

08 · Why we love them

Company, calm, and a reason to get up

Despite the rules and risks, small pets bring company and calm.
