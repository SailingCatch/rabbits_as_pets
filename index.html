A pet on the couch, a pest in the paddock

Rabbits are one of Australia's favourite small pets and one of its worst invasive animals. That double life explains why owning one comes with rules no dog or cat owner ever has to think about.

Rabbits are never far away

Every dot below is a wild rabbit someone reported to the Atlas of Living Australia. They cluster where people live and farm, which is exactly where most pet rabbits live too.

Where you're allowed to keep one

In Queensland, keeping a pet rabbit is illegal under the Biosecurity Act 2014. The maximum penalty is 500 penalty units, about $83,450. Everywhere else, rabbits can be kept as pets, though some places add rules of their own.

The wild ones

Why should a pet owner care about wild rabbits? Because they carry the diseases that kill pets, and because they're the reason the rules exist.

When small pets need a new home

The RSPCA doesn't count rabbits on their own. It groups them with other small pets such as guinea pigs, ferrets, mice and birds under “other animals”. These charts use that group, so they tell us about small pets including rabbits, not rabbits alone.

A disease that crosses the fence

Rabbit calicivirus spreads from wild rabbits to pets through insects, contaminated grass and even shoes. Vaccines protect against it, but there is no vaccine for myxomatosis allowed in Australia, because it could spread into the wild rabbits it's meant to control.
